# 🌿 GP Receptionist Skills Manual 

⭐ Introduction 

The GP Receptionist Skills Manual defines the professional identity, ethical responsibilities, and core competencies required to perform the role of a medical receptionist with safety, consistency, and excellence. It is not a list of tasks. It is a structured framework that explains how a receptionist thinks, decides, communicates, and acts within the ethical boundaries of Australian general practice. 

General practice reception work is a frontline healthcare role. Every interaction—whether administrative, interpersonal, or safety‑related—carries ethical weight. Receptionists are responsible for protecting patient autonomy, privacy, dignity, fairness, and safety while managing workflow, triage, boundaries, and communication. This manual provides the skills and decision structures needed to perform those responsibilities confidently and consistently. 

Each domain contains: 

Ethical Workflows — the ethical tasks within that domain 

Professional Judgement — domain‑specific decision‑making 

Ethical Decision‑Making — how ethical pillars apply to that domain 

Core Competencies — the essential skills for excellence 

Together, these components form a complete, integrated skills framework that supports safe patient care, protects the clinic, and empowers the receptionist to perform their role with clarity and confidence. 

 

🌿 Domain 1 — Communication Skills 

Domain Summary 

This domain explains how receptionists communicate in ways that protect autonomy, dignity, fairness, privacy, and safety. It covers trauma‑informed communication, de‑escalation, and ethically safe information delivery. 

Ethical Workflows 

Autonomy‑Safe Communication 

Communication must offer genuine choices and avoid steering patients toward decisions. Skills: 

Offering choices without steering 

Neutral, non‑coercive phrasing 

Explaining options clearly 

Respecting patient preferences 

Non‑Maleficent Communication 

Receptionists avoid causing harm by using safe, non‑clinical phrasing and avoiding interpretation of symptoms. Skills: 

Avoiding clinical reassurance 

Avoiding interpretation of symptoms/results 

Safe, non‑minimising phrasing 

Avoiding harmful assumptions 

Justice & Fairness 

Communication must be consistent and unbiased for all patients. Skills: 

Consistent tone 

Equal clarity 

Neutral policy communication

Person‑centred language 

Avoiding judgemental tone 

Preserving dignity during conflict 

Cultural safety

Privacy‑safe wording
